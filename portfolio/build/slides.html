The problem

Predict the sale price of a house from 79 descriptive columns

  • Data: 1,460 houses with prices, 1,459 to predict (Ames, Iowa, 2006–2010)
  • Features: sizes, quality grades, neighbourhood, ages, garage, basement… many missing values that mean "feature absent"
  • Metric: RMSE of log price (RMSLE) — errors are relative, so 0.12 ≈ a typical 12 % price error
  • Small data: validation noise is as large as most model differences

My goal was not the leaderboard.
It was a number I could defend: every score measured on data that played no part in producing it.

Starting point: my own exploratory notebook with 17 documented cleaning rules and no model.

Validation protocol

Three layers of data: chosen scores are too good

development · 1,241 rows
holdout

1 · Selection folds — KFold(5, shuffle, seed 42): dark = validation part

2 · Fresh folds — RepeatedKFold(5×3, seed 7), used once to rank 8 finalists

3 · Holdout — scored once, after the model was frozen in Git

  • Holdout first: 219 houses (15 %) locked, stratified by price
  • Selection folds: every choice made on 5-fold CV
  • Fresh folds: finalists re-scored on 15 new partitions
  • Holdout once: after freezing; code refuses a second call

Rule written before reading results: an ensemble must beat the single model by ≥ 0.001 and > 1 standard error.

No leakage by construction

Anything that learns from rows lives inside the fold

Safe before the split

output for a row depends on that row only
  • TotalSF = basement + 1st + 2nd floor
  • OverallQual × GrLivArea
  • "no garage" when garage area is 0
  • quality labels → ordered numbers
  • log1p of the target

Must be inside the fold

output depends on statistics of other rows
  • LotFrontage ← neighbourhood median
  • which columns count as "skewed"
  • one-hot categories, scaler mean/sd
  • outlier removal (training side only)
  • ensemble weights, hyper-parameters

Every fitted step is part of one scikit-learn Pipeline, cloned and refitted on each training fold; validation rows are only transformed.

Leakage safety is a property of the structure, not of discipline.

Finding 1

Deleting the "famous outliers" made every model worse

Public notebooks delete two huge, cheap houses and report a better score.

Removed from training folds only — validation keeps every house — the error rose by 0.004–0.009 for linear models.

The usual "gain" comes from deleting hard houses from the validation data: the exam got easier, the model didn't get better.

Registry E041/E042/E023 vs E049–E0547
Finding 2

Selection bias is real — so I measured it

All 8 finalists got worse on folds they were not chosen on: +0.003 to +0.004.

  • Report the fresh-fold number
  • Differences below 0.003 are not wins
  • Paired per-fold differences, honest SE (repeats are correlated)
Selection: KFold(5), seed 42 · Fresh: RepeatedKFold(5×3), seed 78
Finding 3

Tuning plateaued and ensembles were within noise

Optuna (TPE), staged budget: 25 → 75 trials gained 0.0016; 58 of 75 trials within 0.003 of the best.

Ensembles: Lasso–CatBoost residual correlation 0.856. Two-model variance formula predicts a gain of ≈ 0.0004 (break-even ρ = 0.869).

Best ensemble: −0.0010 vs CatBoost, SE 0.003 → a tie. Kept the simpler model by the pre-registered rule.

Learned blend weights and nested stacking never beat a plain average9

Honest limits & next steps

What I would say before you ask

Limitations

  • Top two models are statistically tied
  • Holdout is small (219 houses)
  • Random folds, not time-ordered; one city, 2006–2010
  • Very large / atypical houses predicted poorly

Next steps

  • Prediction intervals (quantile / conformal)
  • Segment features or models for the extremes
  • Time-aware validation and retraining
  • Serve with FastAPI + Docker, MLflow tracking, drift monitoring

Expected error on new houses: ≈ 0.12 RMSLE (≈ 12 % of the price; median holdout error 5.3 %). No leaderboard score — by choice, the leaderboard is one more thing to overfit.

Five rules I took from this project

  1. 1Lock a test set first.
  2. 2Anything that learns from rows goes inside the fold.
  3. 3A score you chose on is biased — re-measure on fresh data.
  4. 4Decide the comparison rule before reading the result.
  5. 5When the data can't separate models, prefer the simpler one — and say so.
